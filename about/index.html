About

What this is

A skill tree is a map of one school subject: every skill and concept as a node, with a line from each node to the ones it builds on. A teacher writes it. A student reads it, ticks off what they have mastered, and copies an instruction for whatever comes next.

This page says what happens, not why it works. The reasoning, the references and the evidence belong in the paper.

Read the method

What actually happens

  1. 01

    The student picks a node

    The whole subject is on screen at once, so the student can see what a skill rests on and choose where to start. Nothing is hidden and nothing decides on their behalf.

  2. 02

    The page writes an instruction

    It is assembled from the teacher's text for that node, the subject's own settings, the teaching instructions shared by every tree, and a language layer. No language model is involved in writing it.

  3. 03

    The student carries it across

    They paste it into whichever AI chat they already use. That is the whole integration: no account to create, no cost per student, no data processing agreement, and no dependence on one vendor.

The teaching is readable

You can read every instruction

What the AI is told to do is not hidden in a product. It is a handful of text files, versioned and open, and a teacher who disagrees with a line can overrule it for their own tree. That is the point rather than a side effect: teaching you cannot read is teaching you cannot argue with.

What it does not do

  • It does not store what a student ticks off anywhere but in their own browser. Nothing about a student's progress reaches this site, or me.
  • It does not call a language model, so it cannot know what the student then asked, or what they were told.
  • It does not mark anything, and it is not a syllabus. A tree is one teacher's reading of a subject, and may be missing something yours needs.

I am Vidar Skogvoll, a senior lecturer at the University of Oslo, where I work on teaching and on how AI is used in science education.
