How to use

How to use a skill tree

Every tree on this site works the same way. You can try each step below in any tree as you read. The clips are recorded in the Mathematics 2P demo tree. The AI chats in them are real conversations with Claude Sonnet: the instruction was copied from the tree and pasted into a new chat, and the replies are shown as they came, only faster. Another AI service, or another try, will answer differently.

01

Practise skills

As a student, you can practise one learning goal at a time. Click a box in the map, and a panel opens with what the learning goal is about and what it builds on.

Press Copy the instruction, open the AI chat you already use, and paste it in. The AI then works with you on that learning goal alone, and asks questions rather than handing you the answers. Press Show the instruction if you want to read what you are pasting first.

When you know it, press Mark as mastered. The ring turns into a green tick, the boxes that build on it get their full colour, and you can see what to take next.

02

The different nodes

Each box in the map is one learning goal, and there are three kinds:

  • Concept (green, slightly rounded): an idea you should be able to explain in your own words.
  • Skill (orange, round): something you should be able to do, such as solving or calculating.
  • Facts (blue, square corners): a list of things to know by heart.

A pale box is not ready yet: something it builds on is not mastered, and the panel says what to take first. You can still open it and practise. A box you have mastered gets a green tick. The panel also shows the type, and on a skill the level of aids, such as Part 1; more on that below.

03

Generate tests

You can make a test from what you have marked as mastered. Open the menu with ☰ at the bottom left and choose Test. The window shows the learning goals the test covers, and you can add or remove any of them. Choose the difficulty, the number of tasks and the form of the tasks, and press Copy the instruction.

Paste the instruction into an AI chat. You get the tasks first, and the answers and an assessment when you ask for them.

04

Track your progress

You can mark a box as mastered straight in the map, by pressing the ring in it. The list icons at the top of a topic mark every learning goal in it at once, or remove the marks again. The menu shows how many of the learning goals you have completed.

Your ticks are saved in this browser, on this device, and are never sent anywhere. As a teacher, keep in mind that the ticks are your own: they show what you have been through, not what your class knows.

05

Find your way around

You can drag to move around the map, and zoom with + and −. The ⤢ button shows the whole tree at once, and pressing it again takes you back to where you were.

In the menu, Learning goals gives one list of the whole course, sorted by topic, and you can mark learning goals as mastered straight from the list. Search (Ctrl+K) finds a learning goal by a word from its name or description, and takes you to it in the map.

06

Plan a lesson

As a teacher, you can get a plan for the next lesson. Open Lesson plans in the menu, choose the learning goals the lesson should cover, and press Make a resource.

Under Lesson plan, choose the type of lesson and the length, and press Copy the instruction. Paste it into an AI chat, and you get a plan for the whole lesson. The same window can also give you a Mini tree or a Simulation: an HTML file your students open in a browser.

07

Why should I learn this?

As a student, you can ask why the subject is worth learning at all. Many trees have Motivation in the menu.

Say where you are starting from, for example “It's hard” or “What will I need it for?”, and copy the instruction. The AI asks what you care about, one question at a time, and ties the subject to it. Paste it into an AI chat when you need a nudge.

08

Aids and the course

You can press the label in the panel of a skill, such as Part 1 or Part 2, to see which aids belong to that level, for example whether a calculator is allowed. A label split into several colours means the learning goal belongs to several levels, and you should manage it under the strictest one.

It opens About the course, which is also in the menu. There you find the tree's language, its size, the levels, and who made it.

Make your own

Make and edit a tree

As a teacher, you can make a tree for a subject that does not have one yet, or change one that does. It all happens on Make your own: nothing is uploaded, and the tree stays in your browser until you download it.

Open Make your own
09

Make a tree with an AI chat

Press Copy the prompt and paste it into the AI chat you already use. The chat asks a few questions about your subject and your students, and gives you a first draft of the tree as a spreadsheet, with a note on what it is unsure about.

Copy the spreadsheet from the chat and press Paste from the AI chat. The tree opens in the editor, ready to change.

10

Start from an existing tree

You do not have to start from nothing. Press Find a tree in the catalogue, search or filter, and press Open a copy. You can also choose Edit the tree in the menu of any tree.

You get your own copy to change. The published tree is not affected.

11

Edit a node

Click a node in the tree on the right, and the Node tab shows everything about it: name, type, topic and learning outcome. The tree updates as you type, and ↶ undoes a change.

Under Builds on, press Pick in the tree and click the nodes it should build on, then Done picking.

12

Add a node

The Nodes tab lists every node by topic. Press + Add a node here under a topic, give it a name, a type and a learning outcome, and choose what it builds on. + New topic adds a new column.

A prerequisite that would make a circle cannot be chosen.

13

Change the instructions

The Instructions tab shows every instruction the tree gives to an AI chat: the practice tutor, the test, motivation, the lesson plan, the mini tree and the simulation. Each is split into parts, such as Role and style.

Press Edit on a part to rewrite it for this tree only, and Save. A changed section is marked Changed in this tree, and Use the published text takes you back.

14

Share it with your students

View shows the tree the way your students will see it. Download the tree gives you one HTML file that opens with a double-click and works without a network, so you can share it in your learning platform.

Download spreadsheet gives you the file you keep: open it here again whenever you want to change the tree. If other teachers could use it, you can submit it to the catalogue.
